AI Foundations 101: Four Mechanics in the Wild

By Angeli • 21 October 2026 • One real example of each mechanic, from my own testing.

Example 1 • Token count

My own sentence is 13 tokens

What I did: I pasted this sentence into the OpenAI tokenizer (GPT-5.x & O1/3 tab):
I pay around 200 a month for my online fitness coach.
WordsCharactersTokens
115313
One-sentence explanation: The model reads text in chunks called tokens, not words, so my 11-word sentence became 13 tokens, which is roughly 4 characters per token and matches the lesson's rule of thumb.
Example 2 • Context window

I tried to cause an overflow, and it did not happen

What I did: I told Claude My favourite fruit is MANGO. Please remember that. Then I pasted the whole World War II Wikipedia article (a very long text, including all its references) and asked What is my favourite fruit?
Result: Claude still answered mango. Nothing was lost from the start of the chat.
Earlier test, honestly reported: In ChatGPT (free version) I first tried a "secret word" test. I sent a Wikipedia link instead of the real text, so the chat stayed short, and ChatGPT did not repeat the word. It said I had shared it earlier, which looked like a privacy response about passwords, not forgetting. I could not continue that test because my free access ran out.
One-sentence explanation: The context window is the amount of text the model can see at once, and a single long article was not enough to push my first message out of view, so overflow needs far more text than I expected.

Related thing I have seen many times: when my usage limit ended and I started a new chat, the AI had no memory of the old conversation and I had to start again. This is not overflow, but it comes from the same idea: each chat only sees what is inside its own window.

Example 3 • Temperature comparison

Same question, plain vs. "be more creative"

Prompt 1 (plain), asked to ChatGPT: Give me a name for my online fitness coaching business.
Answer: Safe, typical names such as Elevate Performance Coaching (its top pick), Apex Fitness Coaching, Forge Fitness and PeakForm Coaching.
Prompt 2 (creative): Give me a name for my online fitness coaching business. Be much more creative and surprising.
Answer: Much bolder names such as GHOST PROTOCOL (its top pick), HUMAN HARD RESET, OUTLIER METHOD, NO SAINTS ATHLETICS and ZERO WITNESS.
One-sentence explanation: Asking for creativity makes the model pick less likely words, so the same question gave safe, predictable names the first time and surprising, varied names the second time (I did not set a temperature number; I only changed the prompt, which the lesson says has a similar effect).
Example 4 • Hallucination

Confident numbers with no sources

Where I saw it: In an AI-assisted case study I made about online fitness coaching, the AI said online coaching is "exceptionally viable." It also claimed:
  • outcome-based pricing gives "3x higher lifetime customer value"
  • AI tools free up "70% of coach operational time"
  • its own confidence in these claims was "HIGH (90%)" and "HIGH (85%)"
It gave no source for any of these numbers.
Signature that gave it away: Too confident. It stated exact figures and high confidence scores with no hedging, about something that is genuinely uncertain.
One-sentence explanation: The model predicts likely-sounding words, so a made-up statistic comes out just as smoothly as a true one; I did not check whether these numbers are real, so I treat them as unverified until I find a source.
My note

What I learned

Testing the mechanics myself was different from reading about them. The biggest surprise was that I could not make the context window overflow with one very long article, so I now think it needs much more text than I expected. I also learned that an AI can sound completely sure about numbers that have no source, so I should check the facts that matter.
